# Colombian coffee industry: market, value chain and economic weight (fact base as of 26 November 2026)

Notes for the report writer:
- **Primary data used.** I downloaded and parsed the FNC's own statistical workbooks, updated November 2026:
  - "Precios, área y producción de café": https://federaciondecafeteros.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Precios-area-y-produccion-de-cafe-Noviembre-2026.xlsx
  - "Exportaciones Octubre 2026": https://federaciondecafeteros.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Exportaciones-Octubre-2026.xlsx
  - Both are linked from https://federaciondecafeteros.org/wp/estadisticas-cafeteras/
  
  Annual totals, monthly series, destination shares and the exporter ranking below are **my own sums and shares from those files**. I label them "computed from FNC workbook".
- **Calendar year vs coffee year.** The FNC reports both: calendar year (Jan–Dec) and coffee year ("año cafetero", Oct–Sep). Always state which one you mean. The famous "14.87 million bags" is the 2024/25 coffee year, not calendar 2025.
- **Units.** Bags are 60-kg bags of green coffee equivalent. A "carga" is 125 kg of café pergamino seco (CPS, dry parchment). "Billones" in Colombian Spanish means 10^12 (trillion) pesos. FNC monthly reports write "billones de dólares" when they mean USD billions (10^9).
- **Two export-value series.**
  - FNC "valor estimado" uses provisional declared values. The FNC itself says it is illustrative and not official.
  - DANE/DIAN is the official series.
  - The two differ materially; see section 1.

## 1. Production and exports: 2023, 2024, 2025 and 2026 YTD; monthly 2026 trend; destinations; specialty share

### Takeaway
Colombia had a historic 2024/25 coffee year: 14.87 M bags, the highest in about 33 years. It then fell 14.9% to 12.65 M bags in 2025/26. Calendar 2026 production through October is 10.06 M bags (−10% y/y), and exports are 9.58 M bags (−11.8% y/y). Export value fell only about 3% (FNC estimate) because prices and the Colombian differential stayed high. On the official DANE series, unroasted coffee export value is −11.5% for Jan–Aug. The US takes about 40% of export volume, North America about 50% and Europe about 29%.

### Inferences
- The 2026 fall is concentrated in **Jan–Mar** (−29% to −36% y/y). Those months are the tail of the "traviesa"/main crop that the 2025 La Niña rains damaged at flowering. The **May–June mitaca** came in strong (+29%/+43%), and Q3–Q4 is mixed. With the strong El Niño declared in October 2026, the 2026/27 main crop is at risk on volume and quality, though dry flowering can concentrate production (see section 5).
- The gap between **FNC estimated value (−3.1% Jan–Oct)** and **DANE official value (−11.5% Jan–Aug, unroasted)** is large.
  - DANE reports coffee by declaration date: only 66.3% of the coffee declared in August 2026 shipped that month, per the [DANE bulletin](https://www.dane.gov.co/files/operaciones/EXPORTACIONES/bol-EXPORTACIONES-ago2026.pdf).
  - The FNC uses provisional declared invoice values.
  - For a pitch, use DANE/DIAN for value and the FNC for volume.
- Implied average FNC-estimated export unit value: about USD 280/bag (2023), 294 (2024), 387 (2025) and about 430–450 per bag (May–Oct 2026). Value per bag is at record highs even as volume falls, because the Colombian differential widened (section 2).
- On FNC volumes, the EU-27 is about 25–26% of 2025 exports: Europe 28.6% minus the UK, Norway, Switzerland and Russia. This is an approximation; the "Otros Europa" line was not split.
- Japan's share has fallen structurally, from 1.27 M bags in 2000 to 0.68 M in 2025. Korea, China, Saudi Arabia (137k bags in 2025 vs 4k in 2015) and Australia are growing (FNC workbook).

### Gaps
- No official national share of specialty coffee in total exports was found for 2025–2026; the 63.4% figure applies only to the FNC's own sales. Any widely quoted national percentage should be treated as unverified.
- The official DANE/DIAN coffee export value for full-year 2025 was seen only as "about USD 6,500 M" (FNC IG). The DANE December 2025 bulletin was not opened.
- The USDA FAS "Colombia Coffee Annual" (May 2026) was not located. The latest USDA Colombia figure found was the Dec 2025 semi-annual, via secondary coverage.
- The September and October 2026 DANE export bulletins were not opened. Jan–Oct 2026 official value is therefore unavailable here.

## 2. Prices: ICE "C" 2024–2026, Colombian differential, FNC internal reference price, exchange rate (TRM), harvest value

### Takeaway
- **2025 was a historic price year.**
  - The ICE "C" contract averaged 368.18 ¢/lb (+56%).
  - It set records above $4.30/lb in February 2025 and about $4.38/lb in October 2025.
  - The FNC internal price peaked at an all-time high of COP 3,380,000/carga on 13 Feb 2025 and averaged COP 2.86 M (+51%).
  - The harvest value reached a record COP 24.4 billones.
- **In 2026 the bonanza reversed.**
  - KC fell to about 270–300 ¢/lb.
  - The peso appreciated about 18–19% (TRM about 3,100–3,200 in Aug–Oct 2026, vs about 3,900 a year earlier).
  - The internal price dropped about 30% y/y to roughly COP 2.0–2.2 M/carga.
- **The Colombian differential surged** from 3.6 ¢/lb (Feb 2025) to about 72 ¢/lb (Sep–Oct 2026), cushioning the fall in dollar prices.

### Inferences
- In 2026, **exchange-rate appreciation explains more of the farm-gate price fall than the ICE price does**. In September the USD ex-dock price fell about 12% y/y but the peso price fell about 30%. For an exporter this means margins and hedging in COP matter as much as KC.
- The near-record differential (about 72 ¢/lb) signals **scarcity of Colombian washed arabica relative to demand**. Buyers pay up for Colombian origin, and exporters with traceable, consistent supply capture that premium. FNC notes say buyers "esperaban menores diferenciales" (expected lower differentials), so there is downside risk if Colombian supply recovers.
- At about COP 2.0–2.2 M/carga the internal price is still historically high in nominal terms (the 2023 average was COP 1.63 M). It is only 19–30% above the FEPCafé average production cost of COP 1.68 M (section 5), so grower margins are thin.

## 3. Structure of the value chain: families, hectares, departments, smallholders, productivity, renovation, exporters, FNC institutions

### Takeaway
Colombian coffee is overwhelmingly a smallholder crop: about 562,000 producers (often rounded to 540,000–556,000 families), 97% of them with 5 ha or less. They farm about 833,000–835,000 ha across 23 departments, mainly Huila, Antioquia, Tolima and Cauca. The planted area is young (6.3 years on average), 88% rust-resistant and highly dense, with productivity of about 21 bags/ha. Exports are concentrated: the FNC alone handles about 21–23% of volume, and the top 10 exporters ship about 74–76%. The leading private exporters are Olam Agro (ofi), Sucafina, Carcafé, Louis Dreyfus, Racafé, Cóndor, SKN Caribecafé, Sucden and La Meseta.

Private exporters' 2026 volumes fell more sharply: −16.2% y/y in August 2026 for "particulares" vs +18.5% for the FNC, per the [FNC Sep 2026 report](https://federaciondecafeteros.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/9.-Informe-mensual-Septiembre-2026-p.pdf). That squeezes private exporters' origination.
- The private segment is led by multinational traders (ofi/Olam, Sucafina, LDC, Sucden, COFCO) alongside large Colombian houses (Carcafé, Racafé, Caribecafé, La Meseta, Cóndor, Laumayer). Value shares differ from volume shares.

- About 97% smallholders, 21 bags/ha and 6.3-year average tree age mean the bottleneck is **labor and the cost per carga**, not plant renewal.

## 4. Weight in the economy: GDP, exports, employment, DANE statistics

### Takeaway
Coffee is Colombia's main agricultural export: about USD 6.2–6.5 bn and 12.9% of total exports in 2025 (official DANE/DIAN). Estimates of its weight are about 1.5–1.8% of GDP and about 20% of agricultural GDP; these are FNC estimates, not official DANE shares. DANE national accounts show coffee shrinking: −2.2% in 2025, −22.1% in Q4 2025, −11.2% in Q3 2026 and −10.9% YTD 2026. That dragged agriculture to −6.7% in Q3 2026, the worst fall since comparable records began in 2005. The DANE price indices (IPC, IPP) show coffee inflation of about 52% in 2025 reversing in 2026.

## 5. Key challenges 2025–2026: climate, rust, labor, fertilizer and costs, EUDR, US tariffs, generational replacement, smuggling and security

### Takeaway
- **Climate swung from La Niña to El Niño.** La Niña rains in 2025 cut flowering. A strong El Niño was officially declared in October 2026, raising risks for the 2026/27 crop.
- **Pests are under control:** rust 5.27%, berry borer 1.85% (mid-2026).
- **Costs are up and prices down.** The FEPCafé production cost is COP 1.68 M/carga (+10.6% y/y), and labor is 69% of cost.
- **Regulation tightens:**
  - EUDR applies from **30 Dec 2026** for large and medium operators.
  - US tariffs: the 10% reciprocal tariff hit coffee from April to November 2025, then coffee was exempted. Coffee is also exempt from a 12.5% surcharge on Colombian goods imposed in July 2026.
- **Structural pressures:**
  - Strong peso.
  - Aging growers.
  - Armed-group extortion.
  - A proposed 19% VAT on green coffee from 2028.
  - A surge in imports.

### Inferences
- **The binding constraints for 2026–2027 are margin and compliance.**
  - At COP 2.0–2.2 M/carga vs a COP 1.68 M cost, with labor at 69% of cost and the peso strong, growers' incentive to harvest, fertilize and sell is weak. FNC notes say producers held sales in September 2026 "a la espera de mejores precios" (waiting for better prices).
  - EUDR plot-level traceability goes live in about 5 weeks, on 30 Dec 2026, for large exporters selling to the EU, roughly 25–29% of volume. Data, geolocation and chain-of-custody capabilities become a competitive differentiator.
- US tariff risk on coffee is currently low (exempt from both the reciprocal tariffs and the 2026 forced-labor surcharge). The forced-labor due-diligence requirement adds a second traceability layer for the US, which takes about 40% of volume.

### Gaps
- No quantified 2026 deficit of harvest labor was found; the 450,000-picker figure is from 2025.
- No official 2025–2026 data on smuggling volumes or on re-export of imported coffee were found. Import growth is documented, but its link to informality is not.
- The Commission's EUDR country-benchmarking act was not opened directly; Colombia's "standard risk" status relies on a secondary source.
- Fertilizer price levels in COP per bag for 2026 were seen only in chart form, without exact values.

## 6. FNC initiatives: Cenicafé, varieties, digital programs (SICA, cédula cafetera, apps, traceability), sustainability, strategy

### Takeaway
The FNC's technical model rests on three pillars: **SICA** (Sistema de Información Cafetera) as the planning tool, **Cenicafé** research, and the **Extension Service**. In 2025 that model delivered the youngest, densest and most rust-resistant planted area in Colombia's history. On the digital side the FNC now has more than 500,000 growers in SICA, about 400,000 cédula cafetera records (387,000 banked growers), a SICA-based EUDR geolocation service with more than 383,000 records, and Cafenlace, an export platform. Cenicafé is already testing **AI for yield estimation and an AI agent** that answers technical questions.

### Cited Findings
- **Technical strategy.** The Gerencia Técnica strategy rests on three pillars: "el Sistema de Información Cafetera (Sica) como herramienta estratégica de planeación; la investigación e innovación de Cenicafé… y la labor permanente del Servicio de Extensión" (SICA for planning, Cenicafé for research and innovation, and the extension service). — [FNC IG 2025](https://federaciondecafeteros.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Informe-de-gestio%CC%81n-2025_FNC.pdf)

### Inferences
- The FNC already owns the core data assets an exporter needs for EUDR and for forced-labor due diligence: SICA farm and plot data, geolocation and the cédula identity system. Private exporters depend on access to those data or on building their own traceability. This is the natural entry point for a data or AI proposal.
- Cenicafé's early AI work (yield estimation, a technical Q&A agent, NIRS origin authentication) shows institutional openness to AI. A pitch can position complementary tools for exporters: origination forecasting, EUDR due-diligence automation, differential and FX risk analytics, and quality and cupping prediction.
